# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own.** Halal collagen for UK Muslims is a narrow beachhead with a hard constraint (no non-halal gelatine or collagen) that mainstream brands rarely address directly.
- **Distribution may already work.** The site shows "4.8/5 · 320+ verified reviews", so customers are arriving and satisfied.
- **Brand is the one moat on offer.** Trust inside a faith community, plus the meals mission, can compound through word of mouth and community networks a generic brand cannot copy overnight.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No breakthrough, so no moat yet.** Grass-fed bovine collagen with vitamin C and biotin is a commodity formula. A halal certificate is something any competitor can buy, not a secret. If an established brand or own label adds a halal line, only price is left.
2. **Paid acquisition in a crowded category.** Collagen is heavily advertised. If growth relies on paid social, CAC may exceed what a £14.40–£18 pouch earns back. CAC, repeat rate and gross margin are all unknown (/founder-cfo, /founder-competitors).
3. **Claims and trust risk.** The skin, hair and joint claims must be on the GB health claims register, and the certifying body is not named on the site. For a trust-based brand, one public challenge on either damages its only moat.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells halal-certified collagen to UK buyers today, at what price, and what share do you hold of that small market? (/founder-competitors)
- How did your first 100 customers arrive, and what does a customer cost now by channel? (/founder-cfo)
- What do you know about this buyer that a big collagen brand does not? That is your secret, or you have none yet.
- What stops a copycat in month six: certification, supplier exclusivity, community partnerships, subscriber base?
- What share of customers are on Subscribe & Save, and how long do they stay? (/founder-cfo)
- Why now: is halal-conscious demand for supplements growing, and is there evidence? (/founder-consumer)
- After collagen, which adjacent halal products carry the same trust?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Evidence from /founder-competitors that RizqPure leads (or can lead) the UK halal collagen niche, not just one of many sellers.
2. Unit economics from /founder-cfo showing CAC is paid back within the subscriber lifetime at today's prices.
3. A named, credible halal certifier shown on site and packaging, and every health claim checked against the GB register.
4. A 12-month plan for a defensible asset: exclusive community partnerships, a halal range, or a subscriber base that makes copying uneconomic.
