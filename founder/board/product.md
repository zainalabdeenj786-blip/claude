# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one thing is already clear.** "Halal collagen" fits in one sentence. It is one product in one format at one size, with no sprawling range to tidy up. The Product lens rewards that focus.
- **The format fits a daily habit.** An unflavoured powder goes into tea, coffee or a smoothie the buyer already drinks. A 30-serving pouch matches a monthly subscription, and Subscribe & Save makes the repeat purchase the default.
- **There are signs of a real experience.** The site shows 4.8/5 from 320+ reviews and offers 30-day returns. That suggests buyers who stay. The board still needs to check which platform verifies those reviews.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The trust seam sits at the centre of the product.** For this buyer, the halal certificate is the product. The page we read does not name the certifying body. A halal-conscious buyer who cannot see who certified it, and to what standard (for example, how the animals were slaughtered and how the supply chain is checked), has no reason to switch. One public challenge on this point could undo the brand.
2. **The promise may be legally unsayable.** The site promises "firmer skin", "thicker hair" and "mobility" in 4-6 weeks. As far as the board knows, no authorised GB health claim exists for collagen itself. The authorised claims belong to the vitamin C and biotin. If the promise has to be rewritten, the experience has to make up the difference, and today the copy is doing that work. Confirm against the GB Nutrition and Health Claims register.
3. **The first experience has friction and a diluted story.** One pouch costs £18 and free delivery starts at £30, so a first-time buyer probably pays for postage. Even two subscribed pouches (£28.80) fall short of the threshold. The charity element ("a share of proceeds") is also vague. A second message weakens the first unless it is specific and provable.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which body certifies the product, and can a buyer see the certificate in one tap from the product page?
- What do the first five minutes feel like: opening the pouch, the scoop, how it dissolves, any taste or smell in hot and cold drinks? What do the 1-3 star reviews say?
- Which of the site's claims are on the GB register, word for word?
- What share of proceeds goes to meals, through whom, and how does a buyer see the result?
- What would you cut? Should the charity message, the biotin or the hair and joint claims go, to sharpen "the collagen you can trust is halal"?
- What does the unboxing and day-30 follow-up look like, and where are subscriptions cancelled?
- Open, for /founder-cfo: unit cost, gross margin, the delivery cost absorbed under £30, and subscriber retention. Open, for /founder-competitors: which halal collagen brands already reach this buyer, and do they name their certifier? Open, for /founder-consumer: whether the charity element moves buyers or only adds noise.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the certifying body and link the certificate on the product page and the pack.
2. Rewrite every claim so it is on the GB register, and keep the promise honest.
3. Fix the first-order seam. Either make delivery free for subscribers or reprice so one pouch reaches the free-delivery threshold, once /founder-cfo confirms the margin allows it.
4. Make the charity element specific and auditable (a stated share and a named partner), or cut it.
