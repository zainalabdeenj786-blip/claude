# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A clear buyer with a real reason to choose.** Halal-conscious buyers who avoid standard gelatine and collagen are a defined group, reachable through community, mosque, modest-fashion and creator channels. "Halal" is a reason to pick this brand that does not depend on being cheaper.
- **Effort is low.** One scoop of unflavoured powder in a drink each day. Little is asked of the buyer.
- **Some proof already.** "4.8/5 · 320+ verified reviews" and a business that is already trading. Subscribe & Save fits a product people use daily.
- **The meals-for-families give-back** fits the buyer's values. It is a real bonus that costs little per order.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Weak likelihood, and the claims behind it may not be allowed.** The skin, hair and joint results named on the site are the whole dream outcome. Collagen itself has no authorised GB health claim. If those claims have to come off, or the certifying body for "halal" cannot be named and checked, the two things that create belief go away together. Without proof the buyer can trust, this is a commodity.
2. **The guarantee does not cover the promise.** Results are promised in "4-6 weeks", but returns end at 30 days. The buyer carries the risk at the exact point where the outcome is supposed to arrive. That weakens the main reason to buy now.
3. **A commodity stack in a want market, not a pain market.** Skin and hair are a desire, not urgent pain. Apart from halal, the stack is collagen + vitamin C + biotin at £18, a mix any competitor can copy. If a bigger brand gets halal certification, the only lever left is price. Whether this market is growing, and how big it is, are open questions (/founder-competitors).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which body certifies the product as halal, and can a buyer check the certificate in one click?
- Which of your claims are on the GB register (vitamin C for collagen formation, biotin for hair and skin), and can every claim be rewritten to fit them?
- What do one pouch and one subscriber cost and earn: unit cost, CAC, margin after the 20% discount, and how many months subscribers stay? (/founder-cfo)
- What share of buyers subscribe, and why do people cancel?
- Would your buyers pay £25+ for a stronger offer? (/founder-consumer, then /founder-pricing)
- Who else sells halal-certified collagen in the UK, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name and link the halal certifier on the product page. Rewrite every health claim to fit the GB register, with legal review.
2. Make the guarantee as long as the promise. Example: a 60-day "see it or your money back" offer for subscribers, priced in by /founder-cfo.
3. Build the stack around the problems on the way to the result: a 6-week results tracker or photo diary, reminders to take it daily, recipes for mixing, and a visible record of meals funded per order. Then retest the offer at a higher price (/founder-offer).
4. Bring unit economics showing subscriber lifetime value covers acquisition cost at the 20% discount before any spend to scale.
