# RizqPure: competitors and the gap

Read 2026-10-07. I used public pages only and link every fact. A price marked "search index" came from the search engine's copy of the page because the page itself was blocked (403) or loaded its content with JavaScript, so I could not read it. Check those prices before you use them in ads or pricing.

**How I compared prices.** RizqPure gives 10g of collagen per serving, so I priced every product as **£ per 30 x 10g of collagen (300g)**. Some brands use a different serving size (5g, 8g, 13g or 20g). In those rows I also show the price for 30 of the brand's own servings.

## 1. What RizqPure itself shows the public

- **Halal certifier: none named.** The product page says "Fully halal certified" and answers questions with "Contact us and we will share our certification" ([product page](https://rizqpure.com/products/rizqpure-halal-collagen)). The home page says "100% halal-certified" and "halal-slaughtered, grass-fed bovine", but names no certifier either ([home](https://rizqpure.com)).
- **Reviews:** the "4.8/5 · 320+ verified reviews" figure appears only on the site ([home](https://rizqpure.com)). Trustpilot has no profile at uk.trustpilot.com/review/rizqpure.com (the page returns 404). I found no Amazon UK listing and no other trace of the brand in web searches ([search of Amazon and Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rizqplus.com) returned only similar names such as Rizqplus and Rizq Rise). A shopper cannot check the reviews anywhere else.
- **Delivery wording conflicts.** The home page says both "Free Standard Shipping" and "Free UK delivery over £30" ([home](https://rizqpure.com)). One £18 pouch is under £30. Beauvyn ([shop](https://www.beauvyn.com/collections/all)) and Sunna ("Free 24H Tracked Shipping", [home](https://sunnasupplements.com)) both advertise free delivery with no minimum.

## 6. The gap RizqPure can own

**"Halal you can check, and a subscription that respects you, at £15-18."**

The evidence:
- The largest complaint theme across the halal leaders is subscription billing (23 reviews). Reviewers read it as a breach of trust by a faith-branded company. RizqPure already offers Subscribe & Save. It can promise the opposite: an email reminder before every charge, cancellation in one click, and no box pre-ticked for subscription.
- Halal proof is the market's promise, yet only Beauvyn names its certifier on the page. Sunna's certificate is being questioned in public. The theme is thin (2 reviews), but it carries more weight per review than any other.
- No other halal bovine powder I found combines a named certifier, vitamin C and biotin, and a price under £20. Beauvyn names its certifier but costs £24.99 and has no vitamin C. Sunna's powder has no vitamin C, and its vitamin C product is a flavoured drink. Wellgard and BB Pure are cheaper but name no certifier and contain collagen only.

To be candid, **RizqPure does not hold this gap today.** It names no certifier ("Contact us and we will share our certification"). Its 320+ reviews cannot be checked off-site. Its delivery terms contradict each other. All three can be fixed in a week: show the certificate and state the slaughter method (HMC and HFA differ on stunning, per [HalalSpy](https://halalspy.com/halal-knowledge/products/halal-collagen/)), open a Trustpilot profile, and offer free delivery on one pouch. Until then, Beauvyn holds the high-proof position. The vitamin C angle is weak evidence (1 review) and easy to copy, so it should not be the lead message. The meals-for-families pledge may set RizqPure apart, but I did not check whether competitors make a similar promise.

## 7. The threat: who could copy fastest, and how

1. **Sunna Supplements, the fastest and most dangerous.** It has about 12.8k site reviews, Amazon and Superdrug distribution, and a subscription price of £20 per 250g (£24 per 30 x 10g). It already sells a collagen product with vitamin C. It could launch an unflavoured collagen with vitamin C and biotin and publish its certificate within one product cycle. It also files formal oppositions against UK trade marks that use "Sunna" or "Sunnah", according to a [Trustpilot reviewer](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/sunnasupplements.com?stars=1&stars=2&stars=3) who points readers to the UK IPO register (I did not check the register). RizqPure should confirm that its own name is registered.
2. **Wellgard, on price and reach.** It already has "Halal" in its Amazon title, 20K+ units bought a month and a price of £12-15 per 30 x 10g. If it named a certifier, it would undercut RizqPure on Amazon, where RizqPure does not sell.
3. **Beauvyn, on positioning.** It sells the same 300g, 30-serving, grass-fed format, names The Halal Trust and launched three months ago. One price cut to £18-20, or adding vitamin C, would take away RizqPure's differences. Its weakness is very little social proof so far.
4. **Holland & Barrett and Nature's Horizon.** Halal collagen is already on the H&B shelf. An own-brand halal tub would put store distribution behind the category.
