# Marketing: RizqPure

Built from the buyer panels (`panel/results.md`, `panel-v2/results.md`), the board (`board.md`), the offer (`offer.md`) and the CFO's numbers (`cfo.md`). Competitor positioning comes from `competitors.md`.

**What the competitor map changes:** only one halal collagen names its certifier on the page (Beauvyn, The Halal Trust, £24.99, launched July 2026, 18 site reviews). Sunna leads on reach (about 12.8k reviews) but its certifier isn't on the page and its biggest complaint is subscription billing. Wellgard sells 20k+ a month on Amazon at £12–15 with "Halal" in the title and no certifier. The empty spot is **halal you can check, plus a subscription that respects you, at £15–18**. RizqPure fits it on price, but not yet on proof.

## 0. Fix the claims first (before any spend)

The site today says "Radiant skin & glowing hair", "Stronger joints for prayer", "smoother, firmer, more hydrated skin", "thicker, fuller-looking hair", with results in "4-6 weeks" and "3 months" for joints. Only authorised claims from the [GB NHC register](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/great-britain-nutrition-and-health-claims-nhc-register) can be made, and the board found no authorised claim for collagen itself. The claims you *can* build on come from your two vitamins (check the exact wording and the minimum amounts per dose in the register):

| ingredient | authorised-style wording to check in the register |
| --- | --- |
| Vitamin C | "contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin" (also bones, cartilage, gums, teeth, blood vessels) |
| Biotin | "contributes to the maintenance of normal hair" / "normal skin" |

Then:
- **Remove or rewrite** "stronger joints", "firmer skin", "thicker hair" and the timelines as claims for the product. Describing *who it's for* ("for sisters who want to look after their skin", "for the knees you kneel on five times a day") isn't the same as promising a result, but the line is fine. Have a regulatory consultant or Trading Standards Primary Authority check the final copy.
- **Show the vitamin C and biotin amounts** per dose on the page (the claims need a minimum amount).
- **Reviews:** customer reviews that make health claims ("my joints are better") can't be used in ads. Keep them on the review platform and don't feature them.
- **Creators** are bound by the same rules, and must label posts #ad.

## 1. Positioning

Evidence: the "who certified it?" objection blocked 14 of 19 buyers in round 1. Naming the certifier (plus a trial) moved the panel from 5% to 60% "would try". Values-driven and strict halal checkers responded most.

Three versions:

1. **For halal-conscious Muslims who've never been sure their collagen is halal, RizqPure is the collagen you can check: certifier on the pouch, certificate one tap away, every batch tested. Unlike mainstream tubs that say nothing, and halal labels that say nothing more.** ← *recommended*
2. For Muslim women who want a beauty routine that's halal down to the last scoop, RizqPure is the halal collagen with vitamin C for normal collagen formation, unlike beauty brands that never answered the gelatine question.
3. For families who give and want their spending to give too, RizqPure is the halal collagen where every pouch funds meals.

Recommended: **1**. It answers the objection that blocked the most buyers, it's true without stretching claims, and a big brand can't copy it quickly without a credible certificate. Use 2 as the ad angle for the beauty segment and 3 as the Ramadan angle, not as the brand's core.

**One line for everything:** *Halal collagen you can check.*

**Second message (from the competitor reviews):** *A subscription that respects you: a reminder before every charge, cancel in one click.* Lead with the certificate. Use this as the reason to subscribe rather than buy once.

Don't name or knock competitors in ads. Comparative claims must be fair and verifiable under the CAP Code.

## 2. Channels

The first audience is the segments that responded in the re-test: household decision-makers (6/6), beauty enthusiasts (4/4), values-driven (2/2), strict halal checkers (3/4), gym-goers (3/4). The sceptical label readers (0/3) and family-first spenders (0/2) aren't the first audience.

| channel | why it fits | rough cost | how you'll know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Muslim creators (TikTok / Instagram), UK, 10k-100k followers** — beauty, modest fashion, mum life, gym | 5+ panel buyers said a creator they follow would flip them | gifted product + £100-£500 a post, or affiliate 15-20% (EST) | unique code per creator; trial packs sold per code |
| **WhatsApp sharing** — "share with your sisters" link in every order | buyers named family WhatsApp groups as their trusted source again and again | the referral reward (e.g. £5 credit each) | referral link orders |
| **Independent reviews (Trustpilot / verified on-site reviews)** | many said reviews on the brand's own site "don't count" | free tier; the review-request email | review count and rating on an independent platform |
| **Mosques, Islamic centres, sisters' circles, Muslim gyms** — sampling with trial packs at events | trust + the halal-certificate story land in person | trial packs + stall fees | QR code per event |
| **Paid social (Meta/TikTok), retargeting only at first** | to convert people creators already warmed up | capped at £10-20/day (EST) | cost per trial and cost per pouch; stop above the cap below |

**Not doing yet:** Google search ads (low volume for "halal collagen", and expensive), cold paid social at scale (the CFO what-if shows a £6 marketing cost per pouch wipes out the profit), Amazon (comes after the certifier is visible and reviews exist; several panel buyers asked for it, so it's the next step, not never).

## 3. The 30-day campaign: "The 7-Day Halal Collagen Trial"

Launch day L = the day the new pouch, certificate page and trial pack go live. Dates are relative, so pick L in `launch.md`. If L falls near Ramadan, use angle 3 in week 3.

| day | channel | what goes out |

| L-12 | TikTok/IG | Founder video: "I couldn't find a collagen I was sure was halal, so…" (no claims) |

| L+2 | Creators | First creator posts (#ad) |

When there's nothing new to say: packing day, a certificate audit, the founder answering a halal question from a DM, what's in the scoop (10g, the amounts of vitamin C and biotin), meals delivered with the partner.

### Ten hooks (each answers a panel objection)

1. "Who certified your collagen? Turn the tub round. I'll wait." (trust)
2. "Halal on the front. Certificate number on the back. Check it yourself." (trust)
3. "Not 'no pork gelatine'. Halal from source to pouch." (trust; check this is literally true for your certificate)
4. "£4.95. Seven days. See if it disappears in your chai." (price, effort)
5. "No subscription trap. Try it for a week first." (lock-in)
6. "Lab tested, every batch. Results on the site." (trust, quality)
7. "Your sisters' WhatsApp group asked. Here's the certificate." (trust, word of mouth)
8. "For the knees you kneel on five times a day." (joints audience; describes the buyer, makes no claim. Check the wording)
9. "Every pouch funds meals for families. Here's the running total." (values)
10. "Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin. That's the claim. That's all we'll say." (sceptics; honesty as the hook)

## 4. Budget and numbers

**The most you can pay to win a customer.** From the CFO: each pouch leaves £9.28 before marketing (£6.28 contribution + the £3 marketing already in it). An average order is 1.75 pouches (your even mix), so a first order is worth about £16 before marketing. If a customer buys about 3 pouches in total (an ASSUMPTION: replace it with your real repeat rate), they are worth about £28. To keep about half of it, **cap cost per new customer at about £14**, and cost per trial at about £14 × your trial → first-order conversion (at 30%, about £4.20 per trial).

**Monthly split (EST, at about £600/month on marketing):**

| channel | £/month |
| --- | ---: |
| Creator gifting + fees/affiliate | 250 |
| Trial packs for sampling and events | 150 |
| Retargeting ads | 150 |
| Referral credits | 50 |

**Three numbers to watch weekly, and when to change something:**

| number | change something when |
| --- | --- |
| Trial → first pouch conversion | under 20% after 3 weeks: fix the day-5 email and the mixing experience first, not the ads |
| Cost per new customer (all marketing ÷ first orders) | over £14 |
| Subscriber month-2 retention | under 60% |

Panel quotes are research, not testimonials. Never use them in ads.
