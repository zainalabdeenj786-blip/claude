## What would flip a no

- Seeing the named halal certification body and the certificate, and someone in my family WhatsApp group saying they've used it and it worked.
- The certifying body named clearly on the pouch and the site, one I recognise like HMC or HFA, with the certificate I can check. Then I'd buy one pouch, not the subscription.
- Someone in the family WhatsApp group saying they've used it and it helped, or seeing it on offer on Amazon with independent reviews.
- Seeing the named certifying body (one I recognise, like HMC or HFA) with a certificate I can verify on the certifier's own website, plus a full source and abattoir breakdown.
- Finding out my current collagen definitely isn't halal, plus seeing RizqPure on Amazon at a similar price per gram with Prime delivery.
- The certifying body named on the pouch with the certificate on the site, ideally plus a Muslim creator I trust actually using it for a few weeks.
- Independent third-party lab test results for the actual product plus a link to decent clinical studies on collagen peptides at this dose, so I'd know it does something.
- A cheap trial size, like a week's worth for a few pounds, so I can see if it actually mixes into my tea fine and helps my recovery before committing to £14-18 a month.
- The certifying body named on the pouch and site (one I recognise, like HMC), plus a cousin in the WhatsApp group saying it actually helped her knees after a couple of months.
- Seeing it on Amazon or in Holland & Barrett at a proper intro offer, say under £12 a pouch, with reviews I can trust.
- Independent clinical studies on this specific dose, plus the halal certifying body named clearly so I can check it myself.
- Independent clinical evidence for the actual product or dose, plus the halal certifier named so I can check it myself, and ideally a cheaper trial size so I'm not committing to a full pouch.
