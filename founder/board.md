# Board meeting: RizqPure

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score 5.0 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 6). Memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **The halal certifier is not named on the page we read.** All three said this. For this buyer, the halal certificate is the product. If they can't check it, they have no reason to switch, and one public challenge could undo the brand.
2. **The health claims are probably not allowed in GB as written.** All three said this. "Firmer skin", "thicker hair" and "mobility" in 4-6 weeks are claimed for collagen, and no authorised claim exists for collagen itself. The allowed claims belong to the vitamin C and biotin. Rewriting the claims removes the copy that is doing the persuading today.
3. **The formula is a commodity, so there's no moat yet.** Offers and Monopoly said this. Any brand can buy a halal certificate. If a big collagen brand adds a halal line, only price and brand are left.
4. **Unit economics are unknown.** All three said this. Nobody knows the cost per pouch, the cost to win a customer, the margin after the 20% subscription discount, or how long subscribers stay.

## Where they disagreed

- **The charity element.** Offers counts it as a real, cheap bonus. Product says it is vague and dilutes the one message: make it specific and checkable, or cut it. The panel can settle this.
- **How much the market itself is the problem.** Monopoly scores lowest because there's no secret and no technology moat, only brand. Product scores highest because the one-product, one-format focus is right and the experience fits a daily habit.

## Conditions (the checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] Name and link the halal certifier on the product page and the pack (all three). → `/founder-brand`, `/founder-ops`
- [ ] Rewrite every claim to fit the GB Nutrition and Health Claims register, with a legal or regulatory check (all three). → `/founder-marketing`
- [ ] Unit economics: margin per pouch at £18 and at £14.40, cost to win a customer, subscriber lifetime value pays back acquisition (Offers, Monopoly). → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Make the guarantee as long as the promise, e.g. 60 days for a promise of 4-6 weeks, costed (Offers). → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Fix the first-order gap: one £18 pouch is under the £30 free-delivery threshold (Product). → `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Make the meals giving specific (a stated amount and a named partner) or cut it (Product). → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-consumer`
- [ ] Evidence that RizqPure leads, or can lead, the UK halal collagen niche (Monopoly). → `/founder-competitors`
- [ ] A 12-month plan for something hard to copy: community partnerships, a halal range, a subscriber base (Monopoly). → `/founder-launch`, `/founder-plan`

## The strongest version the board can see

RizqPure is the collagen a halal-conscious Muslim can buy without a second thought. Its certifier is named on the front of the pack and the certificate is one tap away, and its claims are ones the law allows and the buyer can believe. It sells mainly on subscription, at a price where the first order ships free, with a guarantee long enough to cover the time results take, and a giving promise you can count ("every pouch = X meals via <partner>"). The moat isn't the powder. It's trust built through community (mosques, sisters' WhatsApp groups, Muslim creators, Ramadan) faster than a big brand can buy a certificate. This is narrower than "collagen for skin, hair and joints". It is "halal you can verify", with skin and hair as the reason to start.
