# Brand: RizqPure

## 1. The name: keep it, protect it

RizqPure is already trading with 320+ reviews, so renaming would throw away trust. The job is to check and protect the name, not replace it.

- **What it says:** *rizq* (sustenance and provision from Allah) + *pure*. Halal and clean, instantly, to this audience. It reads well on a pouch and a 32px icon ("RP" or a single mark).
- **Risk:** "pure" is a common supplement word, so the mark's strength sits in "Rizq". Other businesses use "Rizq" (food, charity, finance), so check for conflicts in your classes.

**Checks to finish yourself** (public searches only; a trademark attorney confirms, and nothing is protected until it's registered):

| check | where | what to search |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UK trademark | [UKIPO search](https://trademarks.ipo.gov.uk/ipo-tmtext) | "RIZQPURE", "RIZQ PURE", "RIZQ", in **class 5** (dietary supplements) and **class 29/30/32** (food and drink powders) |
| Register it if not done | [Apply for a UK trademark](https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark) | word mark "RIZQPURE" in class 5 (+29 if you'll sell foods), from about £170 for one class online (check the current fee) |
| EU / export later | [EUIPO eSearch](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/) | same terms |
| Domains | rizqpure.com (yours); check rizqpure.co.uk | buy .co.uk if it's free, to stop copycats |
| Handles | Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, WhatsApp Business | same handle everywhere |
| Confusion | `competitors.md` | no competitor name or mark close to "Rizq" |

## 2. Promise, taglines, voice

**The promise (what a customer can count on every time):** *Halal you can check, in every pouch.* The certifier on the front, the certificate one tap away, the lab results published, and the giving counted.

**Tagline options:**
1. **Halal collagen you can check.** ← recommended (straight from the panel's top objection)
2. Halal from source to scoop.
3. Pure provision. Proven halal.

The current tagline, "Halal collagen that benefits you and others", is warm but makes two promises at once (the board's Product lens flagged this). Keep "and others" for the giving section, not the headline.

**Voice: three words.**

| | do | don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transparent** | "Certified by [body], certificate no. [x]. Here's what it covers." | "100% halal guaranteed!!" with nothing behind it |
| **Warm, like a sister or brother** | "Try it in your chai for a week." | Clinical lab-speak, or influencer hype |
| **Modest** | "Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for normal skin." | "Glow up", "anti-ageing miracle", before/after photos |

**Before/after rewrite (a line from today's site):**
- Before: "Radiant skin & glowing hair. Stronger joints for prayer."
- After: "10g of halal collagen a day, with vitamin C for normal collagen formation and biotin for normal hair and skin. For the life you live, and the prayers you make five times a day."

(Check the final wording against the register.)

## 3. The look: a brief

**Colour** (choose to match what you already have if it works; the hex values are a starting point):

| role | colour | hex | job | contrast note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Deep emerald | `#0F4D3A` | trust, halal, calm; the pouch body | white text on it ≈ 10:1 (passes AA) |
| Warm | Sand / date | `#E8D9C0` | warmth, the "sister" voice; backgrounds | use dark text (`#1E1E1E` ≈ 13:1) |
| Accent | Gold | `#C99A3B` | the certificate seal and the "check it" button only | don't put small white text on it (≈ 2.6:1, fails); use dark text |

Run every final pair through a WCAG contrast checker. AA needs 4.5:1 for body text.

**Type:** a clean serif for headlines (e.g. *Fraunces* or *Playfair Display*, both free on Google Fonts under the OFL) and a plain sans for body text (*Inter* or *Source Sans 3*, also OFL). For an Arabic word like رزق on the pack, use *Amiri* or *Noto Naskh Arabic* (both OFL), and check the spelling with someone fluent.

**Logo brief:** must work as a 32px app icon, on a pouch front and in black and white on a Royal Mail label. Wordmark "RizqPure" + a simple mark (a drop or a leaf in a crescent-free form; avoid religious symbols, which some buyers find inappropriate on commercial goods). Must not resemble any certifier's logo. If you want concepts generated, I can make some with an image tool; they're concepts, not a finished logo.

**The certificate seal (the most important design element):** a front-of-pack panel, same place on every pack: "Halal certified by [BODY] · Cert no. [x] · Scan to check" + a QR code to the certificate page. Use the certifier's logo only as their licence allows.

## 4. The first five touchpoints

1. **The product page:** headline "Halal collagen you can check", then the certificate panel *above* the price, then the trial pack as the first button.
2. **The pouch and the trial pack:** the certificate seal on the front; the vitamin amounts and the authorised claims on the back; a mixing line ("one scoop in chai, coffee or a smoothie").
3. **The order confirmation:** "Your order is packed with batch [x]. Lab results here. Certificate here. [Meals partner] receives [£x] from this order."
4. **The first social post on launch day:** the founder turning a pouch round to show the certificate number, then checking it live on the certifier's site.
5. **The reply to the first "is it really halal?" comment:** within hours, in public, with the certifier, the number, the scope and the stunning question answered plainly. Never defensive.
