## 3. What you're allowed to claim (UK) 🛡️

This is the most important commercial constraint for every ad you run.

- **Collagen itself has no authorised health claims** in the UK (GB Nutrition & Health Claims Register). Lines such as "collagen
  reduces wrinkles / stops hair loss / heals joints" are **unauthorised**. The ASA regularly rules against them, and Meta rejects ads that make them.
- **Vitamin C does have authorised claims**, and your pouch contains it. You may say (wording can be adapted only if the meaning stays the same):
  - "Vitamin C contributes to normal **collagen formation** for the normal function of **skin**"
  - …of **cartilage** (→ joints), **bones**, **gums**, **teeth**, **blood vessels**
  - "Vitamin C contributes to the reduction of **tiredness and fatigue**"
  - **Condition:** the daily serving must provide **≥ 15% NRV = at least 12 mg Vitamin C**. *(Please confirm the amount, see Questions.)*
- **Biotin** would unlock "contributes to the maintenance of normal **hair** / **skin**". That only applies if biotin is actually added (≥ 7.5 µg per serving).
- **General benefit wording** (e.g. "supports skin, hair & nail vitality", which is on your pack) is allowed **only when an authorised
  specific claim sits next to it**. Pair it with the Vitamin C line on every ad and page.
- **Pain points are fine to name; promises to fix them are not.** "Brittle nails? Dull skin?" (naming the problem) is OK. "Fixes brittle nails in 4 weeks" (promising a result) is not.
- **Testimonials count as your claims.** "My hair was falling out in clumps… RizqPure helped" is a medicinal-style claim made on your behalf. Keep only testimonials that describe taste, routine, texture, service and values, or ones that stay within authorised claims.
- Avoid **medicinal** language entirely: *treat, cure, heal, prevent, relieve pain, arthritis, hair loss, alopecia, postpartum hair loss*.

**Compliant formula for every ad:**
> **[Pain point the audience feels]** → **[RizqPure fits your routine, halal you can trust]** → **[authorised claim: Vitamin C supports collagen formation for normal skin / cartilage / bones]**

*This is a summary, not legal advice. Before scaling spend, a one-off check of your site and ads with a regulatory consultant or your local Trading Standards (Primary Authority) is worth the cost.*

## 5. Pain points RizqPure helps with

Evidence rating = strength of the **published research on hydrolysed collagen** (not on RizqPure specifically). "Say it like this" = wording that stays within the claim rules above.

### 5.1 Ageing skin: fine lines, loss of firmness, dryness
- **Felt as:** "My skin looks tired", "I look older in photos", makeup sitting in lines, dryness under hijab and AC, mirror anxiety at 30+.
- **Evidence:** **Moderate.** A 2023 meta-analysis of 26 randomised trials (n≈1,700) found improved skin hydration and elasticity at 2.5–10 g/day over 8–12 weeks. Effects are modest, and a 2025 *American Journal of Medicine* review argues the evidence isn't strong once industry-funded studies are excluded.
- **Say it like this:** "Your daily glow ritual. Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of skin."
- **Hooks:** "Your skin's collagen drops every year after 25. Your routine shouldn't." · "Skincare from the inside of your mug."

### 5.2 Ramadan & fasting skin
- **Felt as:** Dehydrated, dull, flaky skin during long summer fasts. Little time to eat well at suhoor and iftar. (A review on your site already mentions this.)
- **Evidence:** Indirect; this is a seasonal routine angle rather than a clinical one.
- **Say it like this:** "Add a scoop to your suhoor smoothie or iftar chai. One less thing to think about this Ramadan."
- **Seasonality:** Your highest-intent window each year.

### 5.3 Hair: shedding, thinning, breakage (including postpartum & hijab-related)
- **Felt as:** Hair in the shower drain, thinner ponytail, traction around the hairline from hijab underscarves, postpartum shedding at 3–6 months.
- **Evidence:** **Weak.** There is no good RCT evidence that collagen reduces hair loss. Biotin helps only in deficiency.
- **Say it carefully:** You can name the frustration but **cannot promise thicker hair or less shedding**. If biotin is added: "Biotin contributes to the maintenance of normal hair." Without biotin, keep hair to the general "supports hair vitality" line next to the Vitamin C claim.
- **Note:** Postpartum hair loss is your most emotional testimonial theme and your highest regulatory risk. Use it for community and empathy ("you're not alone"), not for promises.

### 5.4 Brittle, peeling nails
- **Felt as:** Nails snapping, peeling layers, can't grow them out. Frequent hand-washing and **wudu five times a day** dries hands and nails, a uniquely relevant insight for this audience.
- **Evidence:** **Low–moderate.** Small, mostly uncontrolled studies (e.g. a 2017 open-label study with 25 people taking specific collagen peptides) reported faster growth and fewer broken nails.
- **Say it like this:** "Wudu five times a day? Your hands do a lot. Give them some love." Then the general nail-vitality line with the Vitamin C claim.

### 5.5 Joint stiffness: kneeling, sujood and standing in prayer ⭐ *(your most ownable angle)*
- **Felt as:** Knee pain going into sujood, stiffness sitting for tashahhud, older parents moving to praying on a chair, aching after taraweeh, gym knees.
- **Evidence:** **Moderate** for knee osteoarthritis and activity-related joint discomfort (several meta-analyses report modest pain reduction over 3–6 months). But "joint pain relief" is a **medicinal** claim you can't make.
- **Say it like this:** "Made for a life of standing, bowing and kneeling. Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of cartilage."
- **Why it's powerful:** No mainstream collagen brand can talk about *prayer*. It is emotional, specific and true to your values. It also opens up Segments C and E.

### 5.6 Bones: menopause & ageing
- **Felt as:** Fear of osteoporosis, especially women 45+ and family history.
- **Evidence:** **Emerging.** A 2018 RCT in 102 post-menopausal women (5 g/day specific collagen peptides for 12 months) showed improved bone mineral density.
- **Say it like this:** "Vitamin C contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of bones."

### 5.7 Halal uncertainty *(the problem only you solve fully)*
- **Felt as:** Label-checking fatigue. Not knowing whether collagen is porcine, whether bovine was zabiha, or whether gelatin capsules or processing aids are haram. Feeling excluded from mainstream beauty and wellness.
- **Evidence:** Market fact. Much mainstream collagen is porcine or non-certified bovine, and even "halal" listings often don't name a certifier.
- **Say it like this:** "100% hydrolysed halal bovine collagen peptides. Certified by **[certifier]**. Nothing to second-guess."
- **Strength:** This is your strongest differentiator **once the certifier is named**.

### 5.8 Taste, texture & routine
- **Felt as:** Chalky, fishy (marine collagen), clumping, sweetener-heavy flavoured powders; "I always forget"; too many pills.
- **Evidence:** Product fact (unflavoured, dissolves hot or cold).
- **Say it like this:** "No taste. No clumps. Disappears into your chai, coffee or smoothie."

### 5.9 Guilt-free self-spending *(values)*
- **Felt as:** Feeling indulgent spending on yourself; wanting purchases to do good.
- **Fact:** 10% to Islamic Relief, quarterly.
- **Say it like this:** "Your wellness becomes someone else's relief." Publish the quarterly donation figures. Don't build ads around specific crises (e.g. Gaza or Sudan imagery), which can feel exploitative and trigger Meta's sensitive-content review.

### 5.10 Value: "collagen is expensive"
- **Fact:** **£0.80/day** single, **£0.72/day** (2-pack), **£0.68/day** (3-pack): cheaper than a coffee.
- **Say it like this:** "Less than 80p a day."

### Not recommended as angles
Gut health / bloating (weak evidence, appears in a review), weight loss, "anti-ageing", "detox", acne / "clear skin" (a testimonial on your site), cellulite. These are high-risk, low-evidence claims.

## 7. Pricing & offer analysis

| Offer | Price | Per pouch | Per day (10 g) | Real saving | Supply |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 pouch | £20 | £20.00 | £0.80 | n/a | 25 days |
| 2 pouches | £36 | £18.00 | £0.72 | **10%** | 50 days (~7 wks) |
| 3 pouches | £51 | £17.00 | £0.68 | **15%** | 75 days (~11 wks) |

**Recommendations**
1. **Lead with the 3-pack as "The 12-Week Ritual"** (or call it "Full Course"). The research uses 8–12 weeks, and your own FAQ says 4–8 weeks to notice results, so the 3-pack is the honest "give it a proper try" option. Mark it **Best Value**.
2. **Fix the 2-pack label:** either "Save 10%" at £36, or **£34 = Save 15%** (and then make the 3-pack £48 = Save 20% to keep the ladder).
3. **Free-delivery threshold:** The site says free UK delivery over £30 *and* free on all orders. If it's over £30, the single pouch (£20) pays shipping, which naturally pushes customers to the 2-pack. Pick one rule and state it once.
4. **One discount only.** Retire "18% off", "up to 25% off" and "25% off first purchase". Use the bundle ladder plus **Subscribe & Save** (10–15%) for repeat purchases, and keep first-order codes for email capture.
5. **Restock moment:** "Sold out in [X] weeks. Back in stock" is real social proof. Run a waitlist now (email/WhatsApp) and give waitlisters first access.
